## Overview

Vault is a fintech brand built around a single idea: your money is genuinely safe here. Not fast. Not easy. Safe. The brand occupies a gap in the fintech landscape — calm authority, warm premium aesthetics, and a tonal register that takes money seriously without performing startup energy or institutional coldness.

Everything in this kit flows from that core. Every color, font, and word choice should reinforce one thing: weight. The weight of real protection, long-term custody, and earned trust.

## 1. Color Palette

### Primary Palette

| Name | Hex | Role | Usage Notes |
|------|-----|------|-------------|
| **Vault Black** | `#171210` | Primary background | Dark theme base; all main surface areas. Has warm brown undertones — not a cool navy or pure black. This is what the inside of a vault looks like. |
| **Burnished Gold** | `#B89030` | Primary accent | CTAs, active states, key brand moments, wordmark secondary element. The only brand in fintech to own gold as a primary accent color — this is the flag. |
| **Bone White** | `#F3EEE4` | Light base | Light mode backgrounds; large text blocks; primary text on dark surfaces. Warm off-white — not stark digital white. |
| **Vault Slate** | `#2C2520` | Secondary surface | Card and panel backgrounds in dark contexts; elevated surfaces one step above Vault Black. |
| **Warm Stone** | `#8C7B6B` | Tertiary / muted text | De-emphasized body copy on dark surfaces; captions, metadata, secondary labels. |

### Color Rationale

**Why this palette?**  
Else's research found that every dark-themed fintech brand uses cool darks — navy, near-black with blue undertones (Stripe's `#0A2540`, Revolut's Shark). Vault Black's warm brown undertone is a 1-pixel-level differentiation that compounds visually across a full interface. It reads as different without announcing itself.

Burnished Gold (`#B89030`) is completely unclaimed in the fintech category. Brex uses orange (energetic, startup-coded). Monzo uses coral (consumer warmth). Gold sits in a different register entirely — it implies permanence, value that persists over time, and a slight formality. It is the accent color of a company that expects to still be operating in thirty years.

**What to avoid:**  
Blue-purple (Stripe), bright green (Wise/Chime), orange (Brex), hot coral (Monzo). These are occupied colors — using any of them signals you are trying to be someone else.

## 2. Typography

### Heading / Display Font

**DM Serif Display**  
Source: Google Fonts (free, open license)  
Weights available: Regular (400)  
CSS fallback: `'DM Serif Display', 'Playfair Display', Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif`

**Usage:** All headline copy — hero H1, section headers, marketing statements, wordmark lettering.

**Why this font:**  
Else's research found that zero competitors use serif type as a primary visual element. Every fintech brand surveyed uses geometric or neo-grotesque sans-serifs (Inter, GT America, Aeonik). DM Serif Display is a contemporary high-contrast serif — it feels editorial and considered, not antiquarian. It signals permanence and craftsmanship without reading as "legacy bank" because its proportions are modern and its contrast is sharp.

**Risk mitigation:** DM Serif Display reads as premium only when used with whitespace and restraint. Never use it small (under 32px in display contexts). Pair it always with DM Sans to prevent it from looking like a law firm website.

---

### Body / UI Font

**DM Sans**  
Source: Google Fonts (free, open license)  
Weights available: 300, 400, 500, 700  
CSS fallback: `'DM Sans', system-ui, -apple-system, 'Helvetica Neue', Arial, sans-serif`

**Usage:** All UI copy — body text, labels, navigation, form fields, data displays, fine print.

**Why this font:**  
DM Sans and DM Serif Display are from the same type design family (Colophon Foundry). They share optical proportions and weight balance, meaning they pair without visual tension — the serif headlines and sans body text feel like one system, not two fonts bolted together. DM Sans at 400 weight on dark backgrounds is highly legible; at 300 it works well for secondary text without losing readability.

### Type Scale (Recommended)

| Level | Font | Size | Weight | Use |
|-------|------|------|--------|-----|
| Display | DM Serif Display | 56–72px | 400 | Hero headlines |
| H1 | DM Serif Display | 40–48px | 400 | Page titles |
| H2 | DM Serif Display | 28–32px | 400 | Section headers |
| H3 | DM Sans | 20–22px | 700 | Card titles, feature names |
| Body | DM Sans | 16px | 400 | Standard copy |
| Caption | DM Sans | 13–14px | 400 | Metadata, footnotes, captions |
| Label | DM Sans | 12px | 500 | UI labels, tags, status indicators |

## 3. Tagline

### "Where money is kept."

**Four words. No punctuation beyond the period.**

### Strategic rationale

The fintech tagline landscape clusters around two postures:  
- **Promise of speed/ease:** "Banking made easy" (Monzo), "The World's Money" (Wise)  
- **Capability claims:** Revolut's product-feature messaging, Stripe's infrastructure framing

Neither posture is available to Vault without sounding derivative.

"Where money is kept." is a statement of fact, not a marketing claim. It doesn't sell you anything. It doesn't use "easy," "smart," or "seamless." It doesn't end with an exclamation point. It simply describes what Vault is — the place where money is held — with the calm certainty of a brand that has nothing to prove.

The period is intentional. It ends the sentence. That's the voice.

**What it does:**
- Activates the vault metaphor without being literal about it
- Positions custody and protection as the product, not speed or features
- Sounds nothing like any current competitor
- Works as a wordmark companion, footer line, and campaign headline

**Alternatives considered and rejected:**

| Option | Problem |
|--------|---------|
| "Your wealth. Protected." | "Protected" sounds defensive; still marketing-adjacent |
| "Hold what you've built." | Good, but "built" implies audience is builders/founders — limits scope |
| "Safe hands for serious money." | Too wordy for a primary tagline; works as a secondary claim |
| "Keep what's yours." | More personal, less institutional; loses the calm authority register |

## 4. Brand Voice Guide

### Core Posture

**Calm authority.**  
Vault writes like a firm that has been doing this for decades, even if it hasn't. The brand is unhurried and specific. It does not try to sound friendly. It does not use startup energy. It does not hedge with "try to" or soften with "kind of." It makes statements and stands behind them.

The closest reference point in the competitive landscape is Mercury — but Mercury's voice is tuned for founders and carries engineering precision as its brand signal. Vault's calm authority is broader: it speaks to anyone who has money they consider serious, regardless of how they earned it.

---

### Tone in Practice

**Unhurried.** Take the long sentence where another brand would take a headline. Explain the mechanism, not just the outcome. The reader is not in a hurry; don't imply they should be.

**Specific.** Name the number. Name the regulator. Name the policy. Vagueness is what bad financial services do to avoid accountability. Specificity is the trust signal.

**Declarative.** Subject-verb-object. No passive voice. No hedges. "We hold your deposits in segregated accounts" — not "Your deposits may be held in segregated accounts."

**No exclamation points.** Ever. This is a standing rule.

---

### Vocabulary

**Use:**
- hold, keep, protect, preserve, custody, secure, maintain, safeguard
- considered, deliberate, precise, earned, reliable, permanent, durable
- long-term, over time, built to last
- transparent, clear, accountable, specific
- your money, your assets, your deposits (personal but not sentimental)

**Avoid:**
- easy, simple, smart, seamless, frictionless, effortless
- unlock, power, leverage, supercharge, optimize, accelerate
- instantly, immediately, in seconds (implies speed is the product — it isn't)
- game-changing, innovative, revolutionary, disrupting
- we're here for you, we've got your back (emotional register; not Vault's voice)
- excited, thrilled, delighted (no exclamation, no emotional performance)

---

### Voice in Action — Example Phrases

**Example 1 — Onboarding email:**
> "Your account is open. Your deposits are held in segregated accounts at our partner institution, insured to £85,000 per depositor under the FSCS. Nothing moves without your instruction."

*What this does:* States facts in order. No warm language, no celebration. The specificity (£85,000, FSCS) is the reassurance — not "your money is safe with us."

---

**Example 2 — Feature description (interest rate):**
> "3.9% AER, variable. Calculated daily. Paid monthly. No minimum balance to qualify. No lock-in period."

*What this does:* Five sentences. Each one answers one question. The structure is the trust signal — a brand that lists terms this clearly has nothing to hide.

---

**Example 3 — Error message:**
> "We couldn't verify your identity. This is a required step. Contact support and we'll work through it."

*What this does:* No apologetic padding ("We're so sorry!"). No blame shift ("Something went wrong"). States the situation, states why, states the path forward. The voice stays the same in failure states — that consistency is the brand.

---

**Example 4 — Marketing headline (billboard / social):**
> "No fees on the first £1,000 transferred. No conditions attached."

*What this does:* A specific offer stated as fact. "No conditions attached" addresses the skepticism before it forms — not with charm, with specificity. This is how Vault runs promotions.

---

## 5. What Vault Is Not

These are as important as the guidelines above. Brand clarity lives in what you rule out.

- **Not a neobank.** Don't use "banking made easy" language. Don't suggest that Vault's value is simplifying banking — simplicity is Monzo and Wise's territory.
- **Not a startup.** Don't use growth language, velocity language, or "we're building" language in customer-facing copy. Vault is a product that exists, not one that is becoming.
- **Not warm.** Don't write "We love helping people with their money." Vault is not warm. It is trusted. These are different things.
- **Not tech-forward.** Don't lead with infrastructure, APIs, or developer language. That's Stripe's territory. Vault's technology is in service of custody, not the product itself.

| Bone White `#F3EEE4` | Vault Slate `#2C2520` | 13.04:1 | PASS |
| Burnished Gold `#B89030` | Vault Black `#171210` | 6.25:1 | PASS |
| Burnished Gold `#B89030` | Vault Slate `#2C2520` | 5.08:1 | PASS |
| Vault Black `#171210` | Burnished Gold `#B89030` | 6.25:1 | PASS |
| Warm Stone `#8C7B6B` | Vault Black `#171210` | 4.57:1 | PASS (marginal) |
| **Warm Stone `#8C7B6B`** | **Vault Slate `#2C2520`** | **3.71:1** | **FAIL — normal text** |
| **Burnished Gold `#B89030`** | **Bone White `#F3EEE4`** | **2.57:1** | **FAIL — all sizes** |

### Corrections

**1. Warm Stone — update hex to `#9E8E7E`**

Warm Stone is designated for body copy, captions, and labels on dark surfaces — including Vault Slate card backgrounds. At `#8C7B6B`, it achieves only 3.71:1 on Vault Slate, failing AA for normal text.

Corrected Warm Stone `#9E8E7E` achieves **4.76:1 on Vault Slate** and **5.86:1 on Vault Black**. Visual character is unchanged — same warm taupe, ~15% lighter.

**2. Burnished Gold — add a constraint and a new token**

Gold on Bone White achieves only 2.57:1, failing even for large text. Gold must not be used as a text or icon color on light surfaces.

- **On dark surfaces:** continue using Burnished Gold `#B89030` as a background (CTA button, active state). Vault Black text on gold achieves 6.25:1.
- **On light surfaces (Bone White):** if a gold accent is needed as text or icon, use **Deep Gold `#7A5F1A`** — achieves **5.22:1 on Bone White**.

### Updated Token Set

| Token | Hex | Change |
|---|---|---|
| `--color-stone` | `#9E8E7E` | Was `#8C7B6B` — lightened to pass AA on Vault Slate |
| `--color-gold-deep` | `#7A5F1A` | New — for gold text/icons on Bone White only |

### Palette Cohesion

Passes. All five colors share warm amber-brown undertones; Burnished Gold operates as a singular accent without breaking palette cohesion. The corrected Warm Stone preserves the tonal ramp.

### Typography

Passes. DM Serif Display + DM Sans is a sound same-family pairing. Minimum viable sizes (H2 at 28px, caption at 14px, label at 12px at weight 500) are appropriate. No changes required.
